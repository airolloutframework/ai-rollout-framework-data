## The AI Readiness Score

*Source: [ai-readiness-score](https://airolloutframework.com/ai-readiness-score)*

**What does the AI Readiness Score measure?**
Organizational AI capability across four pillars: strategy and
leadership clarity, governance and risk awareness, workflow
integration, and capability and skill development. It produces an
instant stage-based report with recommended next steps.

**Who is the assessment designed for?**
Managers and directors responsible for workflows, teams, and outcomes
inside structured organizations. It is not a technical exam — no
coding knowledge is required.

**How long does it take?**
16 questions across four pillars, approximately 3–5 minutes. Results
are calculated instantly in the browser.

**How do you measure AI readiness?**
By scoring the conditions around AI adoption rather than the technology
itself — leadership clarity, governance, workflow fit, and team
capability.

## The AI implementation roadmap

*Source: [ai-implementation-roadmap](https://airolloutframework.com/ai-implementation-roadmap)*

**What are the phases of the AI implementation roadmap?**
Three stage-gated phases over 90 days: Establish Clarity & Guardrails
(Days 1–30), Introduce a Controlled Pilot (Days 31–60), and Measure,
Formalize & Scale (Days 61–90). Each phase closes with a leadership
decision gate.

**What are the stages of AI implementation?**
Organizational, not model-building: (1) establish ownership, governance,
and a readiness baseline; (2) run one controlled, measured pilot on a
real workflow; (3) measure before/after, formalize what worked, and
scale responsibly.

## Governance: policy and ownership

*Source: [workplace-ai-policy](https://airolloutframework.com/resources/workplace-ai-policy/)*

**Who should own AI policy — IT, legal, or operations?**
Operations, in most organizations — whoever is closest to the workflows
where AI is actually used. IT advises on tool security, legal advises
on liability and regulation, leadership signs off on boundaries. But
one named person, usually in operations, should be accountable for the
policy being current, known, and followed.

## Governance: unauthorized ("shadow") AI use

*Source: [employees-using-ai-without-approval](https://airolloutframework.com/resources/employees-using-ai-without-approval/)*

**What is shadow AI and why is it a problem?**
AI tools being used inside an organization without official approval,
IT knowledge, or governance oversight. It's a problem not because
employees are using AI, but because unauthorized use carries real risk:
sensitive data may be entering tools with no data handling agreement,
AI outputs may be going unreviewed into decisions or client-facing
work, and the organization has no visibility into what's happening. The
solution isn't prohibition — it's structured visibility and governance.

## Pilots: the scaling trap

*Source: [ai-pilot-success-scaling](https://airolloutframework.com/resources/ai-pilot-success-scaling/)*

**What is the "AI success trap"?**
It happens when a successful AI pilot creates so much leadership
excitement that the structure that made it work gets abandoned in favor
of speed. When organizations scale too fast, guardrails get loosely
applied to different workflows, the people who made the pilot succeed
get spread thin, and when something goes wrong, AI itself takes the
blame rather than the pace. Recognizing the pattern before you're in it
is the best protection against it.

## Measurement: what to track

*Source: [ai-roi-measurement-framework](https://airolloutframework.com/resources/ai-roi-measurement-framework/)*

**What metrics should you track for AI adoption?**
Time spent on specific tasks before and after AI assistance, error
rates in AI-assisted workflows versus baseline, team adoption rates and
confidence levels, quality scores for AI-assisted outputs, and
leadership confidence in the adoption direction. Track metrics that
connect directly to the business outcomes leadership cares about.

## Rules of thumb referenced in the roadmap

*Source: [ai-implementation-roadmap](https://airolloutframework.com/ai-implementation-roadmap)*

**What is the 30% rule in AI?**
A rule of thumb that AI should handle roughly 70% of repetitive,
data-heavy work while people keep the remaining ~30% for judgment,
oversight, and decisions. It's a guideline, not a regulation — a
reminder that AI augments capability rather than removing the human
accountability the governance layer depends on.

**What is the 10/20/70 rule for AI?**
AI success comes roughly 10% from algorithms, 20% from technology and
data, and 70% from people and processes — the clearest case for a
capability-first, not technology-first, rollout.
